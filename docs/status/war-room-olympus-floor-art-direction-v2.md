## Non-negotiable theme priority

1. **Mythology / history / Olympus first.** The first-glance read must be an ancient Greek / Hellenistic command hall: temple architecture, stone craft, bronze/antique-gold inlay, Greek key discipline, ceremonial strategy-room gravity.
2. **Strategic command chamber second.** It may feel prepared for a war council or campaign map table, but those elements must be added later as separate transparent station/prop layers, not baked into `floor_base.png`.
3. **Hermes/JARVIS third and subtle.** Cyan signal energy, abstract wing/caduceus rhythm, and faint relay-like glow may appear only as restrained accents integrated into stone seams, edge channels, or thin inlay lines. They must not dominate the room.

If a candidate looks like a cool sci-fi dashboard room with a few Greek decorations, reject it.

## floor_base visual target

Create only the empty architectural shell for Olympus Command:

- 16:9 landscape, direct overhead or very shallow orthographic top-down perspective.
- Empty walkable floor and wall boundary only; enough open center and side zones for later modular stations.
- Premium museum-quality / strategy-game environment art, not interface mockup art.
- Ancient Greek / Hellenistic material language: dark marble, carved limestone/stone slabs, bronze and antique-gold inlay, temple plinth geometry, subtle Greek key border work, worn-but-regal floor craftsmanship.
- Olympus atmosphere: ceremonial, powerful, mythic, historic, high-altitude temple-command feeling without literal gods or statues.
- Lighting should feel like torchlit marble and restrained divine/cyan signal glow, not neon cyberpunk or glass dashboard ambience.

## Required mythology/history anchors

The floor base should include several of these, while staying empty and modular:

- Greek key or meander borders along edges and thresholds.
- Carved stone floor slabs with believable age, seams, wear, and polish.
- Bronze/antique-gold thin inlay channels in geometric temple patterns.
- Marble wall or parapet boundary suggesting an ancient command hall or temple precinct.
- Hellenistic symmetry, ceremonial thresholds, plinth-like corners, and restrained sacred geometry.
- Subtle abstract Hermes cues: wing-like line rhythm, caduceus-inspired twin-channel curves, or messenger-signal pathways, all non-textual and secondary.
